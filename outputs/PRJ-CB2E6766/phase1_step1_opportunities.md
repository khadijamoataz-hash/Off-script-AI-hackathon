### Sharpened idea: Hydration Log for Desk Workers

- Problem: They forget to hydrate during long work sessions, leading to physical symptoms (headaches, dry mouth) and reduced cognitive performance, because generic 'every 2 hours' reminders are ignored as noise.
- Product: A simple mobile app that sends context-aware reminders based on the user's last logged drink time (not a fixed schedule) and tracks intake via quick taps, costing $0 to build using no-code tools.
- Why these scores: Problem severity is high (dehydration causes real pain). Market is huge and accessible. Founder fit is low because they have no dev skills and zero budget, but the 'cheap_first_test' score is 5 because this specific niche can be validated with a free Notion template or Google Form in one hour, requiring $0 spend.

### Alternative 1: Output-Based Hydration Tracker

- Problem: They cannot rely on thirst or fixed schedules; they must log bathroom visits to ensure adequate flushing of the kidneys and prevent infection recurrence.
- Product: A digital diary app focused solely on logging voiding times and volume, with optional clinician sharing features.
- Why these scores: Problem severity is highest (medical necessity). Market is smaller and harder to reach than general workers. Founder fit remains low due to lack of skills/budget, but testing via a simple form is feasible.

### Alternative 2: Gamified Aquarium Tracker

- Problem: They fail to maintain hydration because the task feels abstract and unrewarding, lacking immediate visual feedback.
- Product: An app where logging water adds a fish to an aquarium, with gamified levels and streaks.
- Why these scores: Problem severity is low (habit building is easier than medical necessity). Market is reachable but competitive. Founder fit is poor; building a gamified app with custom assets requires significant time and design skills the founder lacks.

**Researcher would test first:** Hydration Log for Desk Workers. This option targets the founder's constraint of $0 budget and 1 hour/month by focusing on a problem that can be solved immediately with free tools (like Notion or Google Forms) rather than building a native app. It addresses the specific failure mode identified in sources: generic reminders are ignored, whereas context-aware logging solves the 'forgetting' issue without requiring complex algorithms.

## Critic review

**Strongest case:** The core problem of dehydration among desk workers is real and well-documented, creating a genuine need for better tracking than generic advice. The market is underserved by high-quality, non-intrusive solutions, as most existing apps are cluttered with ads or rely on ignored fixed-schedule reminders.

**Risks**
1. Zero budget and zero technical skills make building even a simple no-code MVP impossible within the founder's stated constraints of $0 and 1 hour/month.
2. The target market is too broad; 'everyone worldwide' at $10/month is an untestable assumption that ignores cultural differences in hydration habits and payment willingness.
3. Churn will likely be immediate because users who forget to drink water are unlikely to pay a subscription to track it, especially when free alternatives exist.

**Kill criteria (stop if any is true)**
- Inability to build a functional prototype within the first week using available free tools.
- Zero paid sign-ups after offering the solution to 20 potential desk workers for free or at a deep discount.

**Cheapest test that could disprove the idea:** Interview 10 remote workers about their current water habits and willingness to pay $10/month for a reminder app, without building anything.

**Changes to consider (options, not applied)**
- Pivot to a free, ad-supported model with optional one-time purchase for premium features. Reason: Users are highly skeptical of monthly subscriptions for health tracking; a freemium model lowers the barrier to entry and tests actual engagement without risking revenue.
- Niche down specifically to corporate wellness programs or HR departments. Reason: B2B sales allow you to bypass individual payment friction, though this requires sales skills the founder currently lacks.

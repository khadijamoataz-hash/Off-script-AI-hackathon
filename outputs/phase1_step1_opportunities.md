## Critic review

**Strongest case:** The market pressure is real: AI has displaced junior coding tasks, and visa restrictions block international CS graduates from traditional internships. QA testing remains a viable entry point because it relies on technical reasoning rather than pure code generation, offering a logical pivot for these desperate candidates.

**Risks**
1. International students may lack the specific soft skills or communication abilities required for QA roles that involve bug analysis and reporting, regardless of AI tool proficiency.
2. Employers may view 'AI-assisted' QA as a temporary stopgap rather than a career path, leading to high churn where graduates immediately quit once they secure a dev role.
3. The 'micro-course + template' model fails if the actual bottleneck is not knowledge but access to legitimate hiring channels or visa sponsorship, which a course cannot solve.

**Kill criteria (stop if any is true)**
- Zero interviews scheduled with international CS graduates after 2 weeks of cold outreach.
- Less than 10% of interviewed candidates express genuine interest in QA roles over development roles despite the market pressure.

**Cheapest test that could disprove the idea:** Post a free, high-quality LinkedIn post or Twitter thread offering a 'Free AI-QA Resume Review' specifically targeting international CS grads; if fewer than 5 people apply within 7 days, the demand is non-existent.

**Changes to consider (options, not applied)**
- Pivot to 'AI-Driven DevOps Internship Prep' focusing on cloud infrastructure monitoring rather than manual testing. Reason: DevOps roles often value automation logic over manual bug finding and may be less saturated than entry-level QA.
- Target local US bootcamps or university career centers to offer a 'white-label' workshop instead of a direct-to-consumer course. Reason: Bypassing the need for individual sales by embedding the solution into existing trust networks where students already congregate.

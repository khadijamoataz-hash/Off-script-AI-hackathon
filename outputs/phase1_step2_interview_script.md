# Phase 1, step 2: customer interview script (The Mom Test)

Project `PRJ-479A6665`. Option being tested: **AI-Augmented QA Tester Onboarding**.

The founder runs these interviews with real people. Foundry does not simulate interviewees, and no interview results are recorded until the founder enters them.

## Who to interview

International Computer Science graduates currently enrolled in or recently graduated from US universities who are actively looking for software engineering jobs but have encountered visa restrictions (like OPT expiration) or rejections due to AI automation concerns. Find them via university career center mailing lists, LinkedIn groups titled 'International CS Students' or 'STEM Visa Jobs', Reddit communities like r/AskAcademia or r/internationalgrads, and alumni networks of specific CS programs.

## Opening

Hi, I'm researching the current job market challenges for international CS graduates in the US. I want to understand your recent experiences with applying for jobs and what actually happened when you tried to get hired. This conversation is just to learn about your situation, not to sell anything.

## Questions (about past behaviour, no pitching)

1. Tell me about the last three software engineering roles you applied for in the past month. What was the specific job title, which company posted it, and what was the outcome?
2. When you received a rejection email or didn't hear back from an employer, what specific reason did they give, or what excuse did you have to make to yourself about why it failed?
3. Describe a time in the last six months when you felt stuck because you couldn't work as a software engineer due to visa status. What exactly were you doing during that period instead of working?
4. What specific actions have you taken recently to try and bypass the 'junior developer' barrier or find a role where your visa status isn't an immediate blocker? Please list the steps you took.
5. Have you ever paid money for a service, course, or consultant specifically to help you land a job or navigate these visa restrictions? If so, what did it cost and what was the result?
6. What is the most frustrating part of your current job search process that keeps you up at night or causes you to delay applying for new roles?
7. If you had unlimited money and no visa restrictions right now, what would be the very first thing you would do differently in your job search strategy?

## Signs of a real problem

- The candidate has already paid for a resume service, career coach, or specialized course specifically to overcome their visa or 'junior' status.
- They describe spending significant time on low-value activities (like endless networking events or building generic portfolios) because they cannot secure traditional interviews.
- They mention specific rejections citing 'visa sponsorship not available' or 'no budget for training juniors' as a hard stop, rather than just 'not a good fit'.
- They express willingness to pay a premium for a solution that guarantees an interview or bypasses the visa hurdle, indicating they are currently stuck and desperate.

## Do not ask

- Would you use our AI academy if we built it?
- How much would you be willing to pay for a platform that helps international students get jobs?
- Do you think an AI-driven approach is better than traditional career coaching for your situation?
